# Style anatomy: ESCAPE VELOCITY

The default look of this playbook, from the film's own records (https://claudia.gallery, the ESCAPE VELOCITY
making-of) and from watching it. 5:06, 1920×1080, a Suno v6 song at 131.5 → 133.9 BPM, 141 shots in 9 chapters, every
word on screen; 188 Midjourney prompts, 636 images, 130 plates; 47 Seedance 2.5 clips + 30 continuations, about $93.

"Slopcore" is the joke and the point: openly AI-made, openly about AI, so dense with real references that it rewards
pausing. It isn't slop because every frame has a reason and every line is explained.

## The premise and the device

A techwear fashion show where every look is a Silicon Valley meme, walked by an AI model who is also a fashion model.
A split-flap board counts down "18 MONTHS TO ESCAPE THE PERMANENT UNDERCLASS" until it flips to "THERE IS NO
UNDERCLASS" and the catwalk lifts off; a LOOK counter numbers the show's looks. One device, one direction, paid off at
the end. Other devices that work the same way: a keynote's slide numbers, a court case's exhibits, a training run's
steps, a departures board.

## The lead

Claudia, held by her identity text (`generation.md`): black blunt bob, one clay-orange streak, a star clip, a headset
mic, a deadpan, curious face. Look 00: a white cropped puff-sleeve shirt, a black pleated coated-nylon skirt with a
harness belt and a hanging garment tag with a barcode, black knee-high boots. She's on screen 83% of the runtime and
sings on camera for 62% of the sung words (plus 17% spoken straight into the lens).

## The picture

Photographic, never illustrated: cinematic 35 mm film stills with fine silver-gelatin grain. The plates' style family:
blue-black `#0f1216` and steel blue, one small red lamp as the only warm note. Places are concrete and carry the joke:
a dark robotaxi back seat, a data-hall catwalk, a front row of open laptops, a blank billboard for "this is not an AI
billboard". Midjourney's own colour is kept; the print pass on top is a colour halftone with line screens, grain,
dither transitions and misregistration.

## The accent

Clay `#D97757`: her streak, the star clip, and the graphics accent (the lit lyric word, counter digits, stamps, chart
lines). No other warm colour on her.

## The layer on top (code)

- **The device:** the split-flap board and the LOOK counter, in every chapter.
- **Lyrics as composition:** subtitles, model cards, garment tags, mastheads and split-flap rows, each word at its
  aligned time.
- **One explaining graphic per line:** a guest list LADIES 0% · GENTLEMEN 0% · AGENTS 100% over a front row of laptops
  each detected as AGENT; a classifier tag flipping BILLBOARD · 0.99 to AI BILLBOARD · 0.97; a +25% = −17% bar chart;
  a seismograph "FEEL THE AGI · M 9.0 · OFF SCALE"; Newton's cannonball for 11.2 km/s; computer-vision boxes tracked to
  her with real labels and scores; a care label; a garment tag with a barcode.
- **Chrome:** a frame HUD with tiny mono labels and timecodes, a ticker of timeline numbers (specs in
  `overlay_system.md`).

## The edit

Mean shot 2.17 s, median 1.83 s (about one bar). By section: intro 1.33 s (one new thing per bar), spoken verse 1.62 s,
sung chorus 2.69 s (lip sync needs room), second verse 2.63 s, bridge 2.25 s, drop 1.58 s plus eighth-note strobes,
outro 4.40 s. Every cut within 0.03 s of a beat or a declared hit; camera moves ride risers into their hits.

## The hook

Her face in a dark back seat with her eyes closed, a red lamp crossing the clay streak, a full-width split-flap row
clattering onto its line and locking on the 1.20 s beat; her eyes open into the lens on the 1.12 s hit. The premise on
screen by 3 s, the first joke by about 10 s.

## The song

Fashion-show electroclash techno: dry analog kick, rolling acid bass, cold detuned stabs, deadpan female spoken verses
rising into a euphoric sung trance chorus, crowd call-and-response, a key change into the last chorus. The lyric is a
collage of the AI timeline's own phrases ("permanent underclass", "feel the AGI", "it's so over / we're so back",
"usage limit"), each one a reference the audience recognises, which is why the graphics work.
