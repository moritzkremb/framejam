# Pitfalls

- **Skipping the silence.** A riser that crashes straight into the drop is a different video. The default cut holds
  black, with no pad tail and no VO, for 1–1.5 s.
- **Silence that isn't silent.** Reverb from the build, a bed that never quite hits zero, or a whoosh that starts
  early. Render the bed, check RMS on that window, and fade the VO out before the gap.
- **Dropping onto more black.** The first frame after the silence is a picture and a hit.
- **Act 1 that never degrades, or act 2 that never cleans up.** The split is the grammar. A light grain over the
  whole trailer is a style, not this playbook.
- **Mocked product UI.** After the reveal, show the real thing (or a real render) in a place. If you don't have it
  yet, hold on B-roll and type — don't invent an app window. That's product-launch.
- **Invented quotes.** No generated celebrity VO, no scraped interviews, no drawn likenesses you weren't given. If
  the user didn't bring clips, don't add "as seen on" faces.
- **Too many words on screen.** One card, 1–3 words. A paragraph over a parking lot is a slide.
- **Type on a busy sky.** Sit the card on the calm part, or dim the plate behind it.
- **CRT for the whole runtime.** Scanlines after the drop make the hero look cheap.
- **A second story after the product.** The last ten seconds are hero + logo, not a feature list.
- **Photosensitivity.** Glitch cuts and flashes stay short and under three full-frame hits a second.
- **Hyperframes video layers.** Every `<video>` needs an id; move a wrapper, not the video element; don't centre
  with `translate(-50%, -50%)` if you also tween x/y.
- **Mixing this with the other launch playbooks.** A 15 s name-on-the-drop sting is launch-teaser. Floating SaaS
  windows and feature proof are product-launch. A founder on camera is founder-launch.
